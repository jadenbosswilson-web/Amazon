Why an $11 Kitchen Scale Will Make You a Better Cook

It's the least exciting item you can buy for your kitchen, and also one of the few that measurably improves your cooking.

Cups lie

A cup of flour can weigh anywhere from 120 to 150 grams depending on how it's scooped and packed. That's up to a 25% difference in one of the most common baking ingredients, before you've even started — which explains why the same recipe can turn out differently every time you make it.

Weight fixes it

Weighing ingredients removes that variability entirely. A gram of flour is a gram of flour, regardless of how it's packed into the container. This matters most for baking, where ratios actually affect texture, but it's also useful for portioning meals and tracking food if that's a goal.

It's also a waste-reduction tool

Weighing out portions before cooking — meat, rice, pasta — tends to reduce over-buying and over-cooking, since you're working from an actual number instead of a guess. It's a small habit that pays for the scale within a few uses.

The bar is low

You don't need a precision lab scale. A basic digital scale with a tare function (so you can zero out the weight of a bowl before adding ingredients) covers everything a home kitchen needs.

Knife Care 101: A 5-Minute Habit That Changes How You Cook

A dull knife isn't just annoying — it's the more dangerous option. Here's the difference between sharpening and honing, explained simply.

Dull knives are more dangerous, not less

It sounds backwards, but a dull blade requires more force to cut through anything, which means more chance of it slipping. A sharp knife under light pressure is more predictable and, counterintuitively, safer to work with.

Sharpening vs. honing — they're not the same thing

Honing (using a honing steel) realigns the edge of a blade that's already sharp; it doesn't remove material. Sharpening actually grinds a new edge and is what you need when a knife has gone properly dull, not just slightly off.

The easy option for most people

How often is 'often enough'

For a knife used most days, a quick pass every few weeks keeps it from ever getting seriously dull, which is a lot easier than trying to rescue a badly neglected edge every six months.

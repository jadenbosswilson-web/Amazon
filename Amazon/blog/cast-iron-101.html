Cast Iron 101: Why a $25 Pan Outlasts Your Nonstick Set

Cast iron has a reputation for being fussy. Most of that reputation is outdated — here's what actually matters.

The appeal

A well-seasoned cast iron skillet develops a naturally nonstick surface, goes from stovetop to oven without a second thought, and — unlike nonstick coatings — doesn't degrade with heat or scratching. A basic 10-inch skillet, cared for reasonably, will outlast several generations of nonstick pans.

What 'seasoning' actually means

Seasoning is just a thin layer of polymerized oil baked into the pan's surface. Most cast iron today comes pre-seasoned out of the box and is usable immediately — you're not required to do an elaborate ritual before the first use.

Care, simplified

The rules people repeat are stricter than reality: hand wash it (a little soap is fine, contrary to the myth), dry it fully, and rub in a light coat of oil every so often, especially after cooking something acidic like tomato sauce. What actually damages a cast iron pan is leaving it wet, not washing it.
